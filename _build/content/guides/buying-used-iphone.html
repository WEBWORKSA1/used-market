A two-year-old flagship phone can cost a fraction of new and work almost as well — if it isn't locked, stolen, financed or on a dying battery. These checks take about fifteen minutes and protect you from every common problem.

Before you meet

  • Ask for the IMEI and check it for blacklist (reported lost/stolen) and unpaid finance with your carrier or a reputable checker.
  • Confirm the exact model, storage and carrier lock status.
  • Compare price with recent sold listings and our Value Estimator.
  • Agree to meet somewhere with Wi-Fi where you can test properly — a phone shop, café or police-station exchange zone.

The must-pass checks

  1. Account lock: the phone should be signed out of the owner's account and erased, showing the setup screen — or the seller signs out and resets it in front of you. If it asks for the previous owner's password, walk away.
  2. IMEI matches the one you checked (Settings → General → About, or dial *#06#).
  3. Carrier status matches the listing: insert your SIM and make a call.
  4. No swelling: the phone lies flat and the screen isn't lifting.

Hardware checks

  • Battery health in settings (iPhone: Battery → Battery Health).
  • Screen: full-white and full-black images for dead pixels, burn-in and tint; swipe everywhere to test touch.
  • Face ID or fingerprint enrolment works.
  • Front and rear cameras, all lenses, flash.
  • Speakers, microphone and earpiece — record a voice memo and make a call.
  • Charging port charges with your cable; wireless charging if applicable.
  • Buttons, switches, vibration motor.
  • Wi-Fi, Bluetooth and GPS connect.

Spotting non-original parts

Repaired phones aren't necessarily bad, but you should know. Some phones show a "parts and service history" or display a notice when a non-genuine screen or battery is fitted. Uneven gaps around the screen and differing colour tones are also clues. Price accordingly.

Paying safely

For a phone worth a few hundred dollars, pay in person after every must-pass check succeeds, or use a tech marketplace where listings are verified and payment is protected. Avoid paying deposits or shipping-first deals with strangers. The Meetup Planner gives you a plan for your price.

Alternatives: refurbished and verified marketplaces

Refurbished marketplaces inspect devices and add a warranty; tech-specific marketplaces review listings and check IMEIs. You'll pay a little more than a private sale but take much less risk. See Refurbished vs used.
