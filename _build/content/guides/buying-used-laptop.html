Used laptops can be excellent value — business-grade machines a few years old often outlast new budget models. The risks are specific and checkable: account and firmware locks, worn batteries and hidden damage.

The lock checks (must pass)

  1. The seller boots it to the login screen, signs out of their account and disables device-finding features — then erases it, or you see it at the setup screen.
  2. No firmware/BIOS password prompt at start-up.
  3. No "managed by your organisation" or remote-management enrolment notice during setup. Ex-corporate laptops sometimes still carry enrolment.
  4. The serial number matches the model and specs advertised.

Hardware checks

  1. Battery health and cycle count (system report or diagnostics).
  2. Every keyboard key and the trackpad, including clicks.
  3. All ports: USB, charging, HDMI, audio, card reader.
  4. Display: dead pixels, backlight bleed, lines, hinge wobble.
  5. Webcam, microphone and speakers.
  6. Wi-Fi and Bluetooth connect.
  7. No cracks, swelling under the trackpad, or liquid-damage marks.
  8. Original charger works and matches the wattage.

Private sale vs refurbished

Refurbished marketplaces inspect devices and offer warranties, which narrows the price gap but removes most of the risk. Tech-focused marketplaces review listings before they go live. Run both prices through Buy Used vs New to compare cost per year of use.
