The headline fee is only part of the story. Platforms charge in different ways, some charge on shipping, some add payment processing on top, and some make the buyer pay instead. Here's how to read them — and what you actually keep.

Three fee models

  • Seller commission: a percentage of the sale, often plus a small per-order amount. Examples: eBay, Mercari, Poshmark, Etsy, Whatnot, Grailed, Reverb.
  • Payment processing only: no commission; you pay card processing. Depop's model for U.S. and U.K. sellers works this way.
  • Buyer pays: you keep 100%; the buyer pays a protection fee at checkout. Vinted is the best-known example; its U.K. help centre describes the fee as typically a percentage of the price plus a small fixed amount.

The shipping trap

Several platforms calculate their percentage on the total the buyer pays, including shipping. If you charge $15 shipping on a $100 item at 13.6%, you pay about $2 more in fees than you might expect. Price shipping at cost, use the platform's discounted labels, and run the numbers in the Fee Calculator with your real shipping cost.

Small-ticket items and flat fees

Flat minimums hurt cheap items. Poshmark's $2.95 flat fee on a $10 item is almost 30%. A $0.40 per-order fee is trivial on $200 and painful on $5. For cheap items, bundle them or sell locally.

Fees you don't see in the headline

  • Promoted listings / boosts — optional, but they can double your effective fee.
  • Instant payout fees — some platforms charge to send money to your bank immediately.
  • Currency conversion on international sales.
  • Returns — on protected marketplaces, "not as described" returns can cost you the shipping both ways.

How to choose

Fees are one input. The best outcome is the highest net at a realistic sale price within your time frame. A higher-fee platform with the right audience often wins. Combine the Fee Calculator with the Where-to-Sell Router, which also weighs speed, audience and trust.

Always confirm

Fees change frequently and vary by category, country and seller level. Use these figures to compare, then check the platform's own fee page before you list.
