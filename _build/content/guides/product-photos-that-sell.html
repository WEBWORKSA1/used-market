Buyers can't touch your item, so photos do the job of an in-person inspection. Good photos don't need a studio — just a routine.

The five-minute photo routine

  1. Clean the item. Dust and fingerprints read as neglect.
  2. Use daylight. Near a window, flash off, avoid direct harsh sun.
  3. Plain background. A wall, a sheet or a floor — no clutter.
  4. Fill the frame and keep the camera level with the item's centre.
  5. Shoot the sequence below.

The shot list

  • Hero shot: the whole item, straight on.
  • Three-quarter angle, back and sides.
  • Labels, tags, serial or model plates.
  • Materials up close: fabric, wood grain, leather, screen on.
  • Every flaw, close up, with a finger or coin for scale.
  • Scale: in a room (furniture) or worn/held (fashion).
  • Proof it works: powered on, battery-health screen, lights on.

Why honest flaw photos sell more

Buyers trust listings that show flaws, and protected marketplaces often side with buyers when an item is "not as described". Showing a scuff up front avoids the dispute and attracts the buyer who's fine with it.

Platform tips

Some platforms now read your photos to auto-fill listing details — another reason to photograph labels clearly. Square photos crop most predictably in search grids. Then write the text with the Listing Builder.
