Second-hand has gone mainstream. Here's what the latest data says — and what it means if you're buying or selling.

1. Resale is taking share from new

ThredUp's 14th annual Resale Report (April 2026) projects the global second-hand apparel market reaching about $393 billion by 2030, growing roughly twice as fast as apparel overall, and describes resale as taking a measurable share from new retail.

2. Most shoppers now buy second-hand

According to the same research, 59% of U.S. consumers bought second-hand apparel in 2025 — up seven points in three years. Buying used is no longer niche.

3. Younger shoppers drive growth

Gen Z and millennials are expected to account for most of the U.S. market's growth through 2030, and nearly half of shoppers discover resale items through social feeds, creators and in-person browsing.

4. AI is reducing friction

Almost half of second-hand shoppers in the 2026 survey said they use AI shopping tools, and platforms are adding photo-based listing and image search. For sellers, clear photos of labels and serials matter more than ever.

5. Fees are diverging

Some platforms dropped seller commissions in favour of buyer fees or processing-only models, while others kept 10–20% commissions. On a $100 sale, sellers keep anywhere from $80 to $100 depending on platform — compare with the Fee Calculator.

6. Tax reporting relief for casual sellers in the U.S.

Form 1099-K reporting reverted to more than $20,000 and more than 200 transactions for 2025 and 2026, so casual decluttering rarely triggers a form. Profit is still taxable. See our tax guide.

7. Safety is the differentiator

Governments now rate marketplaces on identity checks, secure payments and record-keeping, and platforms compete on buyer protection. For you, that means: use protected checkout when you ship, and use the Scam Checker and Recall Checker when you don't.
