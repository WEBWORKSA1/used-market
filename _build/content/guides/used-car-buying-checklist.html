A used car is the biggest second-hand purchase most people ever make, and the private market is where both the best deals and the worst surprises live. Work through this checklist in order: the paperwork checks come first because they can end the deal before you waste a test drive.

Before you go: paperwork and history

  1. Get the VIN (vehicle identification number) from the seller before meeting. Reluctance is a warning sign.
  2. Run a vehicle-history report for accidents, title brands (salvage, flood, rebuilt), odometer readings and ownership history.
  3. Check open safety recalls by VIN on your regulator's site (NHTSA in the U.S.).
  4. Check for liens or outstanding finance where your jurisdiction offers a lookup.
  5. Price it: compare private-party value from a valuation guide with similar local listings. Valuation guides distinguish trade-in, private-party and dealer prices — use private-party.

At the car: walk-around

  1. VIN on the dash, door jamb and title all match.
  2. Panel gaps even; paint colour and texture consistent (repaints suggest accident repair).
  3. Tyres: matching brand, even wear, enough tread. Uneven wear can mean alignment or suspension issues.
  4. Underneath: rust, fluid leaks, fresh undercoating hiding something.
  5. Interior: musty smell, water lines or silt under carpets (flood damage).
  6. Lights, wipers, windows, locks, A/C and heat all work.

Under the hood — cold start

  1. Ask the seller not to start the car before you arrive. A cold start reveals more.
  2. No smoke on start-up; no knocking or ticking that persists.
  3. Oil not milky; coolant clean and at level; no burnt-smelling transmission fluid.
  4. No warning lights left on after start — and check that they illuminate briefly at key-on (a bulb removed to hide a warning is a trick).

Test drive

  1. Drive at city and highway speeds, over bumps, and in reverse.
  2. Brakes stop straight without pulsing or grinding.
  3. Transmission shifts smoothly, no slipping or delayed engagement.
  4. Steering is straight; no vibration at speed.
  5. Listen for clunks over bumps (suspension) and humming that changes with speed (wheel bearings).

Close the deal safely

  1. Independent pre-purchase inspection by a mechanic you choose.
  2. Seller's ID matches the name on the title.
  3. Bill of sale with VIN, price, date, both signatures — two copies.
  4. Pay at a bank where funds can be verified; never by gift card or a wire to a stranger.
  5. Arrange registration and insurance before driving away.

Private-sale car scams

  • The seller is "overseas" or "deployed" and will ship the car after you pay — a classic non-delivery scam.
  • The seller insists on an escrow website they provide.
  • The title isn't in the seller's name ("I'm selling it for my cousin").
  • The price is far below every comparable listing.
