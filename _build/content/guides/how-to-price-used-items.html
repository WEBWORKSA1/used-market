Pricing is where most sellers lose money — either by asking so much that nothing happens, or by guessing low and selling in an hour. Good pricing is a repeatable process, not a feeling.

Step 1: Find sold prices, not asking prices

Asking prices are wishes. Sold prices are facts. On a large marketplace, search your exact model and filter to sold or completed listings. Note the last five sales of the same model in similar condition, and take the median.

Where specialist price guides exist, use them: music-gear marketplaces publish the prices their used gear actually sold for, collectibles databases hold years of auction results, and car valuation guides separate trade-in from private-party values. One music-gear marketplace notes that gear priced in its recommended range usually sells within about a week.

Step 2: Adjust for condition honestly

Buyers discount for uncertainty. Use a standard scale — New, Like New, Excellent, Good, Fair, For Parts — and grade conservatively. The Condition Grader gives you the grade and wording. As a rough guide, each step down the scale costs 10–20% of value.

Step 3: Adjust for completeness and timing

  • Box, accessories, receipts: add a few percent, more for electronics, watches and designer goods.
  • Season: bikes and patio furniture in spring, skis in autumn, textbooks at the start of term.
  • New model launches: phones and laptops drop when the next generation is announced — sell before, not after.

Step 4: Add a negotiation buffer

Most local buyers will offer less. List about 5–10% above the price you'd happily accept. The Value Estimator calculates a list price, a fair middle and a quick-sale price.

Step 5: Check the net after fees

A $200 sale on a 20% platform nets $160; on a 3% platform it nets $194. If you're choosing between platforms, compare the net in the Fee Calculator.

Psychology that works

  • Price just under round brackets ($95, not $105).
  • State "firm" or "reasonable offers considered" — it shapes the first message.
  • Answer the obvious questions in the listing so buyers don't need to ask.
