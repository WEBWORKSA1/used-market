Marketplace scams are not creative. The same dozen scripts appear on every platform, in every country, because they keep working. Safety teams at classifieds sites, consumer agencies and even governments that rate marketplace safety all describe the same patterns. Learn them once and you'll recognise nearly every attempt.

The rule that defeats most of them comes straight from classifieds safety pages: deal locally and face to face — or keep the whole transaction, messages and payment, inside a platform that protects it.

Scams that target sellers

1. Overpayment and "refund the difference"

The buyer "accidentally" sends more than the price — by cheque, fake transfer or reversible payment — and asks you to refund the extra. The original payment later bounces or is reversed, and your refund is gone. Beat it: never refund an overpayment; cancel and ask them to pay the exact amount through the platform.

2. Fake "payment pending — upgrade your account" email

You receive an email that looks like it's from a payment app saying the buyer's money is waiting but you must "upgrade to a business account" or pay a fee to release it. Payment apps don't do this. Beat it: only trust what you see inside your own banking or payment app.

3. The verification-code scam

"I just need to check you're real — I've sent a six-digit code, can you read it to me?" That code lets them hijack your phone number or account. Beat it: never share a code you didn't request for your own login.

4. "My shipper will collect"

A buyer who is overseas, deployed or busy sends a "mover" and pays by cheque or fake transfer, often with extra for the shipper. Beat it: no item leaves until verified money is in your account.

5. Fake QR codes and payment links

You're asked to scan a QR code or click a link "to receive payment". These lead to phishing pages that capture card details. Beat it: you never need to scan or click anything to receive money.

6. Chargeback after pickup

A buyer pays with a method that can be reversed, collects the item, then disputes the payment. Beat it: for in-person deals, prefer cash or money confirmed in your account; for shipped deals, use the platform's checkout and tracked shipping.

Scams that target buyers

7. Deposit to hold an item that doesn't exist

Popular items at great prices "have lots of interest — send a deposit to hold it." The item never existed. Beat it: no deposits to strangers; see it first.

8. Account takeovers and non-delivery

Scammers hijack established accounts with good feedback, post fake listings and accept only bank transfers. Trade Me's safety team describes exactly this pattern. Beat it: pay only with methods covered by buyer protection, and be wary of sellers who insist on bank transfer.

9. Fake escrow

The seller suggests an escrow service — which they built. Beat it: only use escrow services you find and choose independently.

10. Rentals and pets that don't exist

Landlords who can't show the property and will "mail the keys", or puppies that need endless shipping and vet fees. Beat it: see it in person before paying anything.

11. Counterfeits and stolen goods

Designer bags, sneakers, phones and bikes are common targets. Beat it: authenticate, check serials and IMEIs, and use the inspection checklists.

12. Gift cards and crypto

Any request to pay with gift cards or cryptocurrency is a scam. Full stop.

Your scam-proof protocol

  1. Keep all messages in the platform's chat.
  2. Meet in a public, camera-covered place — many police departments offer exchange zones.
  3. Inspect before paying; verify payment in your own app before handing over.
  4. Use platform checkout for anything shipped.
  5. When in doubt, paste the message into the Scam Checker.
