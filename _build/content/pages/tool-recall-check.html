Why recalls matter more when you buy used

New products are pulled from shelves when they're recalled. Used products aren't — they keep circulating on marketplaces, at garage sales and in hand-me-down bags. Regulators publish recalls precisely so owners and buyers can act, and safety-minded marketplaces tell buyers to check recall lists before high-risk purchases like heaters, e-bikes and baby equipment.

How to search effectively

  1. Find the model number and manufacture date on the label — under a crib, on a stroller frame, on an appliance's rating plate, on an e-bike battery.
  2. Search the model number first, then the brand plus product type.
  3. If nothing matches, check the manufacturer's website and the official databases linked in the results.
  4. Inspect anyway: damage, missing parts and modifications can make an un-recalled product dangerous.

High-risk second-hand categories

Infant sleep products and cribs, car seats, strollers, children's toys with small parts or button batteries, helmets, lithium-ion batteries (e-bikes, scooters, power tools), space heaters, and gas appliances.
