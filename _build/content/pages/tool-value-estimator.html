The model, in plain English

Every category loses value along a recognisable curve. Phones drop about a third in their first year as new models launch; mass-market furniture loses most of its value the day it leaves the store; good instruments and iconic designer pieces barely move. We encode that curve for each category as three numbers — first-year loss, yearly loss after that, and a floor — and show them with every result.

Then we adjust for what buyers actually pay for:

  • Condition: New 100%, Like new 90%, Excellent 80%, Good 68%, Fair 50%, Parts 20% of the age-adjusted value.
  • Brand tier: budget −15%, premium +12%, iconic +25%.
  • Demand: ±12% for season and hype.
  • Completeness: up to +12% for box, accessories and proof of purchase.

The output range is ±12% around the middle. The list price adds 8% headroom for negotiation; the quick-sale price undercuts the low end to sell in days.

Always confirm with sold prices

Asking prices tell you what sellers hope for. Sold prices tell you what buyers paid. Search your exact model on a large marketplace, filter to sold or completed listings, and use the median of the last five as your anchor. Category price guides — like music-gear and collectibles price databases or car valuation guides — are even better where they exist.
