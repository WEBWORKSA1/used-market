Buying Used Guitars, Keyboards & Gear: Why Used Is the Smart Default

Quality instruments hold their value, which makes used gear one of the best buys in the market. How to price, inspect and resell without losing money.

By the Used.Market editorial teamUpdated 2026-11-276 min read

In this guide

  1. Price with sold data
  2. Inspect like a tech
  3. When used isn't the deal
  4. Selling gear

Musical instruments are unusual: good ones barely depreciate. That turns the usual used-goods maths upside down — buy used at a fair price and you can often sell for nearly the same later. It's essentially renting your gear for free.

Price with sold data

Music-gear marketplaces record what used gear actually sells for and publish it as price guides with estimated ranges by condition and price history. A retailer-run gear exchange uses its own sales data plus demand, rarity and new prices to suggest used prices. Use these instead of asking prices.

Inspect like a tech

  • Neck: sight down it for twist; check relief and fret wear.
  • Electronics: every pickup position and pot, no crackle.
  • Structure: no cracks, especially at the headstock and neck joint.
  • Tuning stability and intonation up the neck.
  • Serial number consistent with the claimed model and year.
  • Keyboards/synths: every key and control; outputs and MIDI ports.

Use the instrument checklist.

When used isn't the deal

Heavily modified instruments, "vintage" claims with no documentation, and gear with structural repairs should be priced well below comparable originals.

Selling gear

Specialist gear marketplaces reach knowledgeable buyers; local sale avoids shipping risk for amps and fragile instruments. Ship guitars in a hard case with the strings slackened. Compare platforms in the Router.

Frequently asked questions

Do used guitars hold value?

Quality instruments from established makers typically hold value well, and some appreciate. Buying used at a fair price often means you can resell later for close to what you paid.

How do I price used gear?

Use music-gear price guides that publish actual sold prices by model and condition, then compare with current listings.
