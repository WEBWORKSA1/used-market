Refurbished vs Used vs Open-Box: What's the Difference?

What 'refurbished', 'renewed', 'certified pre-owned', 'open-box' and 'used' actually mean, how grades work, and which is the best value.

By the Used.Market editorial teamUpdated 2026-11-275 min read

In this guide

  1. The definitions
  2. How refurbishment grades work
  3. Which is best value?

The definitions

TermWhat it usually meansTypical protection
Open-boxReturned or display item, little or no useOften near-new warranty
Certified refurbishedRestored by or for the manufacturerManufacturer warranty
Refurbished / renewedInspected, repaired and cleaned by a professional refurbisher; graded cosmeticallySeller or marketplace warranty
Used / pre-ownedSold as-is by its previous ownerOnly what the platform provides

How refurbishment grades work

Refurbished marketplaces grade devices by cosmetic condition — typically from "fair" to "excellent" — while requiring all to work fully. One large refurbished marketplace says its verified devices pass a minimum 25-point inspection by industry professionals. Read each seller's grade definitions; "good" on one site may be "fair" on another.

Which is best value?

  • Private-sale used is cheapest but carries the most risk — use our checklists.
  • Refurbished with a warranty is the sweet spot for phones, tablets and laptops for most people.
  • Open-box makes sense when the discount is meaningful and you want near-new condition.

Frequently asked questions

Is refurbished as good as new?

Functionally it can be close, especially with a warranty, but cosmetic condition varies by grade. Read what each grade means on the seller's site.

Who refurbishes devices?

Manufacturers (certified refurbished), specialist refurbishers vetted by marketplaces, and sometimes small repair shops. Warranty length is a good proxy for confidence.
