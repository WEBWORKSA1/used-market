Where to Sell Anything Used: The 2026 Decision Guide

A practical framework for choosing between local apps, shipped marketplaces, instant buyback, consignment and giving it away — by item, value and how much effort you want.

By the Used.Market editorial teamUpdated 2026-11-279 min read

  6. A 60-second decision checklist

There are dozens of places to sell a used item, and each one will tell you it's the best. It isn't — not for everything. The right place depends on four things: what the item is, what it's worth, how fast you need the money, and how much work you're willing to do. This guide gives you a simple way to decide in under a minute.

The five ways to sell anything used

Every resale option falls into one of five models. Knowing which model you're using tells you most of what you need to know about price, speed and effort.

  1. Local pickup apps and classifieds — you list, buyers message, you meet. Usually no fee. Best for bulky, heavy or low-value items where shipping would eat the margin: furniture, appliances, tools, bikes, kids' gear.
  2. Shipped marketplaces — you list, a buyer pays through the platform, you ship with a label. Fees range from zero to about 20%. Best for small, valuable, searchable items: electronics, branded clothing, collectibles, parts.
  3. Instant buyback — you answer a few questions, get a firm offer and ship or drop off. You give up some price for speed and certainty. Best for phones, tablets, laptops, textbooks and cars.
  4. Consignment and managed marketplaces — someone else photographs, lists, stores, delivers and sells; you get a share. Best when your time is worth more than the commission: designer goods, quality furniture, whole closets.
  5. Give it away or donate — zero cash, near-zero effort, and it stays out of landfill. Best below about $20 or when you need it gone today.

Speed, price or effort: pick two

Resale has a classic triangle. You can usually get any two of fast, top price and low effort, but rarely all three.

  • Fast + low effort = instant buyback or a quick local sale priced to move. Expect to give up 20–40% versus the top price.
  • Top price + low effort = consignment. Slower, and the consignor's share might be anywhere from roughly a fifth to three-quarters depending on the item.
  • Fast + top price = a great listing on the right marketplace, which takes effort: photos, research, quick replies.

Decide which corner you're giving up before you list. It stops you from choosing a slow, high-effort platform for an item you need gone by Saturday.

Fees matter less than you think — and more than you think

Seller fees range from 0% to about 20% on the big platforms. On a $100 item, that's the difference between keeping $80 and keeping $100. But a 0% platform where your item sits for two months and sells for $70 is worse than a 20% platform that sells it in three days for $110. Compare net payout at a realistic price, not headline fees. The Fee Calculator shows the net side by side.

Trust: the hidden cost of "free"

Free local platforms usually don't process payment, verify identities or offer protection. That's fine for a $40 lamp handed over in a driveway. It isn't fine for a $1,200 laptop. As the value rises, give more weight to platforms with on-platform payment, identity checks and dispute processes — or follow a strict in-person protocol. The Meetup Planner tells you how strict to be.

A 60-second decision checklist

  1. Estimate the value with the Value Estimator and three sold listings.
  2. Under $20? Bundle or give it away.
  3. Bulky? Local pickup or a managed delivery marketplace.
  4. Small and valuable? Shipped marketplace — or a specialist one if it exists.
  5. Need cash this week? Get an instant offer first, then decide.
  6. Designer or high-value? Authenticated platform or consignment.
  7. Cross-list on two platforms if you can keep inventory in sync.

Frequently asked questions

What's the fastest way to sell used stuff?

Instant buyback (for phones, laptops, books and cars) is fastest because you get a firm offer immediately. For everything else, local pickup apps sell quickest when the item is priced at or slightly below recent sold prices.

Where do I get the most money?

Usually a specialist marketplace with buyers who know the category — a tech marketplace for phones, a music-gear marketplace for instruments, an authenticated luxury platform for designer goods — or a large marketplace with sold-price competition.

Is it worth selling cheap items?

Below about $20, the time spent photographing, messaging and meeting often exceeds the cash. Bundle similar items or give them away locally.
