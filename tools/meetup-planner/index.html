The principles behind the plan

Every serious marketplace safety guide boils down to the same few rules: deal locally and face-to-face where you can, keep conversations and payments on the platform, never pay or accept payment before meeting unless the platform protects it, and meet somewhere public. The planner turns those rules into a concrete plan that scales with the money at stake.

Why the payment advice changes with value

Under a couple of hundred dollars, counterfeit-cash risk is small and convenience wins. As value rises, so does the incentive to fake payments — so the plan moves you toward methods you can verify yourself, in your own banking app or at a bank counter, before anything changes hands.

Frequently asked questions

What is the safest way to get paid in a private sale?

For small amounts, cash counted on the spot. For larger amounts, money that is visibly confirmed in your own bank account before the item changes hands — ideally at a bank branch. Use the platform's checkout when it offers protection.

Are police-station exchange zones real?

Many police departments designate parking spaces or lobbies with cameras for online-sale meetups. Search your city's police website for 'safe exchange zone'.

Is Zelle, Venmo or Cash App safe for marketplace deals?

They work like cash: fast and usually irreversible, with little protection when you pay a stranger. Only pay in person after inspecting, and as a seller only release the item once the money shows in your own app — never because of an email.
